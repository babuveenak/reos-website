01

Executive assessment

No — the current website does not communicate the intended product. But the diagnosis is far more encouraging than "it needs a redesign," because the intended product is already in the codebase. It has simply been disconnected from the navigation.

The repository contains two independent content models that were never reconciled:

What the site leads with

  • app/data/phase1.ts
  • 3 roles: Investor, Buyer, Owner
  • 12 consumer journeys (buy, rent out, renovate, move in, sell)
  • 5 generic steps each, no relationships
  • Powers the homepage, the starter, /stakeholders, the sitemap

What you actually described

  • app/data/reos.ts
  • 24 development stages across 4 phases
  • 8 ecosystems, 8 stakeholders, 11 authorities
  • Typed relations: inputs, outputs, dependencies, entry conditions, bottlenecks, systems
  • Reachable only through the Authorities page

The second model is a genuine first draft of a property-development knowledge graph. It has stable IDs, cross-referenced entities, and a content-governance status field. It is the more valuable asset in the repository by a wide margin. And a visitor arriving at the homepage will almost never see it.

Central finding

The site's link topology is authority-centric — the exact structure your brief rules out — even though its content model is not. The only practical route from the homepage into the 24-stage development lifecycle runs through the Authorities page. You did not build an authority-first site on purpose; you built a journey-first data model and then navigated to it through the authority door.

This means the rebuild is not a rescue. It is a promotion: take the model that already exists, correct three structural flaws in it, and rebuild the navigation and design register around it.

03

What the product should be

Stated in product language, stripped of aspiration: REOS is a structured, sourced, navigable model of how a property development project moves through the UAE regulatory and commercial system — expressed as a graph, entered through intent, and read through whichever stakeholder lens the user occupies.

Three properties distinguish it from every adjacent product:

  • 01It is relational, not topicalCompeting resources answer "what is X?" REOS answers "what does X block, what unblocks X, and who holds it?" The value is in the edges, not the nodes.
  • 02It is sequenced and conditionalContent carries applicability logic — jurisdiction, asset type, height, tenure, route — so guidance narrows as facts are supplied rather than hedging across all cases.
  • 03It is sourced at claim levelEvery operative statement carries an authority, a source, an effective date, and a verification date. This is the moat and the legal shield simultaneously.

What it is not, and should never drift toward: a listing portal, a directory of service providers, a regulatory news blog, or an authority-by-authority reference. Each of these is easier to build, already served by incumbents, and would consume the editorial capacity the graph requires.

05

Assessment of the 12 stakeholder groups

The groups are a good inventory and a flawed taxonomy. The inventory should survive; the taxonomy needs one structural correction that resolves most of the problems you already sensed.

The core defect: three taxonomies wearing one coat

Your 12 groups classify by three different criteria simultaneously — actor type (who someone is), function (what they do), and lifecycle position (when they appear). Group 11 is the clearest tell: "Handover, Property & Facility Operations" is named after a phase, while its members are actor types. Group 6 is defined by function ("who operates project money"), while Group 5 is defined by actor type.

You have already felt this defect twice and patched it twice — the Group 2 / Group 6 bank rule, and the Owners' Association split across Groups 9 and 11. Both patches solve the symptom by duplicating an entity across groups. The underlying cause is that one organisation legitimately plays several roles at different moments.

Recommended correction

Separate Actor (a party type — "commercial bank") from Role (a function played in a specific activity — "escrow account trustee", "project finance lender", "buyer's mortgage lender"). Groups classify actors. Roles attach to activities. One actor may hold many roles.

This single change dissolves the bank problem, the Owners' Association problem, and the future problems you have not hit yet — the developer who is also the master developer, the consultant who is also the engineer of record, the contractor who is also the FM provider under a DBFO structure.

Group 3 should not be a stakeholder group at all

Authorities are not peers of contractors. They occupy a categorically different position: they issue the approvals that gate the graph. Filing them as "stakeholder group 3" flattens the most important asymmetry in the domain — everything else in your model is appointed, negotiated and commercial; this is compulsory, external and dispositive.

Notably, your code already models this correctly. In reos.ts, Authority is a distinct type with its own jurisdiction and source URL, not a stakeholder. Your prose model regressed from your data model. Restore the code's position: Authority is its own entity class, sitting as a rail beneath all four clusters.

  • Group 4 is overloaded. It contains design disciplines, cost management, inspection bodies, testing laboratories and certification bodies.   • Group 12 is a residual bucket. "Cross-cutting" is honest, but valuers and dispute forums are load-bearing at specific gates (valuation gates finance; disputes gate transfer). Anything that gates an activity should be modelled as a role on that activity, not parked in a supporting set.

Keep all 12 as the actor inventory. They are comprehensive and well-observed — better than most industry maps. Change three things: introduce the actor/role split, promote Authority out of Group 3 into its own entity class, and rename Group 11 to something actor-shaped ("Property & Community Operators") so it stops being a phase name.

The Governance & Assurance problem

Grouping 3 + 6 + 7 places the state in the same conceptual box as your law firm and your escrow bank. To a user these are opposites: one is compulsory, external and cannot be chosen; the others are appointed, commercial and can be replaced. Putting them together teaches the wrong mental model at exactly the moment the user most needs the right one.

07

Recommended lifecycle architecture

Your 18-stage prose list is weaker than the 24-stage model already in your code, and both share one flaw that will actively mislead users: they present concurrent tracks as a sequence.

Three problems with a flat stage list

  • Sales and escrow are not stage 11 and 12. In an off-plan project they run parallel to procurement and construction, often starting before the main contract is awarded. Numbering them after construction teaches a sequence that does not exist.
  • Financing is not a stage. It recurs: land finance, the construction facility, buyer mortgages, refinancing at stabilisation. Modelled as stage 5, it disappears from every later moment where it is decisive.
  • 18 items cannot be navigated. Neither can 24. A flat list of that length has no shape a user can hold in memory.

The three-tier structure to adopt

  • TIER 1Phase — 4 to 6, the navigational spineYour code already has this right: Originate · Deliver · Own · Evolve. Add Entitle if you want approvals to read as their own act. This is what appears in navigation and what a user remembers.
  • TIER 2Stage — 18 to 24, the addressable pagesYour existing list. Each is a URL, a page, an SEO target. Never shown all at once outside the explorer.
  • TIER 3Activity — hundreds, the atomic graph nodeThe unit that actually carries dependencies, roles, documents and approvals. This is what your model is missing and what the AI layer will need. "Obtain building permit" is a stage; "submit structural drawings for review" is an activity.

Model tracks, not only sequence

Above the phases, define a small number of concurrent tracks that run across them — Regulatory, Commercial, Financial, Physical delivery. Every activity belongs to one track and one phase. This is what lets you show, honestly, that a sales launch and a piling contract are happening in the same week under different logic, and it is far more informative than any horizontal timeline.

Dependencies then become explicit typed edges between activities, not implied by numbering:

  • blocks — hard prerequisite; the successor cannot legally start
  • enables — soft; the successor becomes practical but is not gated
  • informs — an output feeds a later input without gating it
  • runs-parallel-to — explicit concurrency, so the absence of an edge is not read as sequence

Recommended knowledge graph model

Your proposed chain — Project → Stage → Activity → Stakeholder → Authority → System → Documents → Approval → Dependency → Next — is directionally right but is a path, not a schema. Written as a chain it forces a linear reading of something that branches.

Entity types

EntityRole in the graphStatus today

StageAddressable chapter of the lifecycleExists (24)
ActivityAtomic unit. Carries roles, inputs, outputs, conditions, dependenciesMissing
Actor typeParty class — the 12 groups classify theseExists (8, thin)
RoleFunction played by an actor on an activityMissing

ApprovalThe gate. Has issuer, inputs, validity, prerequisitesMissing — the most valuable gap

ClaimA single sourced assertion with date, authority, confidencePage-level status only

The two additions that matter most

Approval as a first-class entity. Today an approval is implied by a stage name. Modelled properly — with its issuing authority, required documents, prerequisite approvals, validity period and what it unlocks — it becomes the natural join between the regulatory rail and the delivery graph, and the highest-intent page type you can own in search. "What do I need for a building permit in Dubai" is the query; the approval page is the answer.

Condition as a typed predicate on edges. An edge that says "requires civil defence approval" is unusable without knowing when. An edge carrying {jurisdiction: DDA, use: residential, storeys: >G+4} can be evaluated. Without typed conditions, every page must hedge across all cases — which produces exactly the vague content you are trying to escape — and any future AI layer will invent the branch it cannot look up.

Build order

Do not start with a graph database. Author structured content with a schema — typed data files or a headless CMS with relations — and derive the graph. Your README's "CMS-ready, file-backed" instinct is correct. A graph store before the content exists will cost you months and buy nothing; the bottleneck is editorial, not storage.

Recommended user journeys

The platform needs three entry modes, not one. Your brief's example prompts split cleanly into them, and each demands a different first screen.

  • MODE AIntent — "I want to develop a 20-storey residential building in Dubai"A project configurator: jurisdiction → asset type → tenure/sale route → scale. Produces a filtered lifecycle view. This is the flagship, and it should be on the homepage.
  • MODE BIdentity — "I am a consultant; what am I responsible for?"A lens. Same graph, filtered to the activities where that role appears, ordered by phase. Shows what they receive, produce, and hand to whom.
  • MODE CObject — "What is escrow?" · "What is a building completion certificate?"Direct entry to a stage, approval, document or term page — arriving from search, not from your homepage. Most traffic will land here first, so every such page must reconstruct context above the fold.

The universal movement

Whatever the entry, the user must be able to move along the same spine — and, critically, in both directions. Forward answers "what next"; backward answers "why am I blocked", which is the more common and more valuable question in development.

Intent → Context (jurisdiction · asset · route) → Phase → Activity → Roles → Approvals → Documents → Dependencies → Next / Blocked-by

Recommended UI/UX direction

The current design is skilful and wrong for the category. Replace the register, not the craft. Target: an instrument a professional keeps open in a tab — closer to a technical standard or a well-made reference tool than to a development brochure.

Principles

  • Density is a feature. Bring h1 from 128px to roughly 36–44px and section padding from 144px to 48–64px. A professional judges a knowledge platform by how much it can see at once, not by how cinematic the scroll is.
  • Ship the serif, keep a serif. Cormorant Garamond at weight 300 is a display face doing body work; it is poor at small sizes, poor for dense tables, and has no Arabic counterpart for the parity you have already scaffolded. A text serif for reading plus a neutral sans for interface and data will hold authority without the brochure connotation.
  • Retire gold-on-void as the primary system. It is the single strongest signal that this is a luxury sales asset. Reserve one restrained accent for wayfinding and spend colour instead on semantics: status, confidence, gate type, jurisdiction. In a knowledge platform, colour that means something beats colour that sets a mood.
  • Light theme as the default. Reference material is read in daylight, printed, and pasted into reports. Keep the dark theme — it is already built and well-executed — but do not lead with it.

Homepage strategy

Search and discovery strategy

Most users will arrive from a search engine on a deep page, not the homepage. Design for that.

  • Every entity page must rebuild its own context above the fold: which phase, which jurisdiction, which approval gates it, what it blocks. A visitor landing on an activity page from an external search should be able to orient without going up a level.
  • Entity-aware internal search. Results should be typed — an approval, an activity, a document, a role — not a flat list of pages. Filterable by jurisdiction, phase, actor and content status.
  • Question-shaped titles for the highest-intent pages. Approval and document pages should be titled the way the query is phrased, because that is where the durable organic traffic sits.
  • Fix the sitemap first. sitemap.ts currently omits /lifecycle, all 24 stage pages and all 8 stakeholder pages — the most substantive content in the repository is invisible to crawlers. This is a small change with disproportionate return, and it can be made before any redesign.
  • Structured data on every entity page. Typed markup for processes, definitions and how-to steps is how a knowledge graph earns rich results and, increasingly, citation in AI answers.

You already have the beginning of this and should treat it as the product's spine, not its disclaimer. Governance is the moat: anyone can write about Dubai development; almost nobody will maintain a sourced, dated, versioned claim set.

15

Future AI architecture

Your instinct that the architecture should support an AI layer is correct, and the sequencing matters: the graph is the product, the AI is an interface onto it. Build the graph such that an AI cannot answer beyond it.

Four requirements the current model does not yet meet

  • Typed conditions on edges. Without machine-readable predicates, a model asked "does my 20-storey tower need this approval?" will infer the branch. Conditions are what turn retrieval into reasoning.
  • Claim-level provenance. Page-level status cannot support per-sentence citation. If the answer cites, the graph must carry citations at the granularity of the assertion.
  • Temporal versioning. effective_from and superseded_by let the system answer "what applied in 2024", detect its own staleness, and refuse confidently when a claim has expired.
  • Encoded refusal boundaries. The graph must know where it stops. In this domain the most valuable AI behaviour is declining to answer and naming the authority to ask — an unhedged wrong answer about escrow or eligibility is a category-ending event.

The right generation pattern

Not open generation over prose. Constrained assembly over the graph: resolve the user's facts against jurisdiction and asset conditions, traverse the applicable subgraph, and render only what the nodes carry — each rendered claim showing its source chip. The model composes and sequences; it does not supply facts. This makes the same mechanism serve both the trust story and the AI story, and it is far more defensible than retrieval over articles.

The natural progression: filtered journeys (deterministic, no model) → natural-language entry mapped onto the configurator → constrained assembly with citations → saved project state → project dashboards. Each step is useful shipped alone, which is what you want when the underlying data is still being built.

Highest

Editorial capacity, not engineering, is the binding constraint

24 stages × hundreds of activities × jurisdiction variants runs to thousands of claim-nodes, each needing sourcing and periodic re-verification. Dubai alone is a sustained specialist workload. Every finding in this document is fixable in weeks; this one is the multi-year commitment, and it is what will actually determine whether the product exists.

Mitigation: depth-first, never breadth-first. One jurisdiction, one asset class, one route — fully sourced end to end. A single complete, citable spine is worth more than seven emirates at twenty percent, and it is the only version that is demonstrably true.

Highest

Regulatory decay with reputational consequence

Fees change, thresholds move, portals migrate, authority mandates get reorganised. A stale dependency edge that someone sequences a project on is the failure mode that ends the platform's credibility — and dependency edges are precisely the thing you cannot easily spot-check.

Mitigation: publish the graph's shape broadly but gate actionable claims behind a cited source and a verification date. Make status a first-class filter so a user can choose to see only validated content.

High

Audience collapse — already happened once

The consumer funnel is easier to write, easier to rank for, and easier to explain. It out-competed the development model inside your own codebase without a decision ever being made. Unless the audience call in section 09 is made explicitly and defended, the same drift will recur.

High

Regulatory positioning exposure

Real-estate advertising and advisory are regulated activities in the UAE; residency guidance belongs to immigration authorities. The current disclaimers are well-drafted and should be strengthened rather than softened as the platform's confidence grows. This risk scales directly with how authoritative the positioning claims to be — which is the subject of the next section.

High

Complexity outrunning comprehension

A fully-typed graph with conditions, roles, tracks and claim provenance is correct and could easily produce an interface only its author can use. The context bar exists to absorb this: complexity lives in the data, and the interface's job is to narrow it to the user's case before showing anything.

17

Recommended direction

On "Single Source of Truth for UAE Property Development"

Do not use it

It contradicts your own design. You correctly hold that binding actions return to the authority, and your /reos neutrality charter says so explicitly. If the Land Department is the system of record, REOS is by definition not the source of truth — the claim is falsified by your own architecture.

It asserts an authority you do not hold. In a regulated market, claiming to be the source of truth on registration, escrow, off-plan and residency invites exactly the scrutiny your careful disclaimer language is designed to avoid.

It is unsubstantiable on day one. With content at mixed validation status, any domain expert can disprove it in a single session. The phrase makes a promise the editorial model cannot keep.

Stronger alternatives, in order of preference — each is true at launch and gets truer as the graph fills:

  • PRIMARY"The connected map of UAE property development."Claims structure and connection — which is your actual differentiator — without claiming authority. "Map" also correctly implies it points at territory it does not own.
  • ALT"How property development actually works in the UAE."Plainer, more confident, strong in search. Reads as explanation rather than assertion, which is what you are.
  • ALT"The development operating layer for the UAE."Carries the platform ambition. Hold this until there is project state to operate on; used now it over-promises.

Supporting line: REOS connects the authorities, approvals, documents and stakeholders that make property development happen — and links every requirement back to its official source. That sentence claims connection and provenance, not authority, and it is defensible from the first page you publish.

The architecture in one paragraph

A development-first knowledge graph for the UAE. Actors (12 groups, 4 clusters) play roles on activities; activities sit in stages within four phases and run on concurrent tracks; activities consume and produce documents, are gated by approvals issued by authorities on a regulatory rail, and connect through typed, conditional dependencies. Every operative statement is a sourced, dated claim. The user resolves their jurisdiction and asset facts once in a persistent context bar, then reads the same graph through whichever lens they occupy. Later, an AI assembles paths across this graph and may assert nothing the graph does not carry.
